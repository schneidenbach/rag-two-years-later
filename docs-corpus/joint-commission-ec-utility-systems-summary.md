# Internal Summary: Joint Commission Environment of Care Expectations for Utility Systems

## About this Lakeshore internal regulatory summary

This document is Lakeshore Health's internal summary, written in our own words, of what our accreditation surveyors generally look for in the Environment of Care (EC) chapter as it applies to utility systems. It is not a copy of any accreditation standard and does not replace the current published standards, which Accreditation and Regulatory Readiness maintains under license. Facilities Operations, Plant Operations, and Infection Prevention should use this summary to prepare for tracer activity and to understand how Lakeshore procedures support compliance.

## Utility systems management plan expectations at Lakeshore

Lakeshore is expected to maintain a written utility management plan that identifies the utility systems that support patient care (electrical distribution and emergency power, HVAC, domestic water, medical gas and vacuum, plumbing and drainage, steam, and communications), inventories the critical components of each, and assigns maintenance, inspection, and testing activities and frequencies. Surveyors typically review the inventory, the maintenance strategy chosen for each component, and evidence that planned activities were completed on time. At Lakeshore, the utility inventory and activity schedule are held in the CMMS, and completion is reported monthly to the Environment of Care Committee.

## Medical gas and vacuum system maintenance and inspection expectations

Surveyors expect hospitals to inspect, test, and maintain piped medical gas and vacuum systems, including source equipment, alarms, zone valves, and station outlets, at defined intervals. Zone valves must be accessible, clearly labeled with the areas they serve, and not obstructed. Outlets must be tested for flow, pressure, and proper connector function. Repairs and modifications to medical gas systems must be performed by qualified, credentialed personnel, and the system must be verified before it is returned to service. At Lakeshore, only licensed medical gas installers may open zone valve boxes or repair outlets (see the MedGuard MG-4 maintenance excerpt, MAN-MG4-001). Surveyors frequently ask unit staff where the zone valves for their area are located and who to call for an outlet failure.

## Water management program for Legionella and hot water temperature control

Lakeshore is expected to maintain a water management program that identifies areas of the water system where Legionella and other waterborne pathogens could grow and spread, establishes control measures, and monitors them. Key control measures include maintaining hot water temperatures high enough in storage and distribution to limit bacterial growth, while protecting patients from scalding at the point of use. At Lakeshore, this balance is achieved with thermostatic mixing valves, including the ThermaMix TMV-50 valves installed in North Campus Tower B, which blend hot and cold water to a safe delivery temperature. Failed mixing valves must be corrected promptly because they can produce either scald-risk temperatures or lukewarm water that supports bacterial growth. The program also covers flushing of low-use outlets, backflow prevention, and response to out-of-range test results. The Water Management Team, co-chaired by Plant Operations and Infection Prevention, reviews monitoring data quarterly.

## Backflow prevention device testing expectations

Backflow prevention devices, including reduced pressure zone (RPZ) assemblies, protect the potable water supply from contamination. Surveyors may ask for evidence of annual testing by a certified tester and timely repair of failed devices. Lakeshore performs annual RPZ testing as P4 Scheduled work and treats a failed test as P2 Urgent under FAC-POL-002. Only certified backflow technicians may repair and retest an RPZ device.

## Documentation of utility system failures and disruptions

Hospitals are expected to have procedures for responding to utility system disruptions, including who to notify, how to obtain emergency repair services, and what clinical interventions to take when a utility fails (for example, portable oxygen or suction). Surveyors expect utility failures to be documented, investigated, and reported to the Environment of Care Committee, with corrective actions tracked to completion. Lakeshore documents utility failures in the utility failure log and in the CMMS work order. Patterns of repeated failures or prolonged repair times for the same system are expected to be analyzed and addressed through the performance improvement process.

## Survey readiness actions for Lakeshore utility systems

1. Confirm the utility component inventory is current, including Tower B specialty components.
2. Verify medical gas zone valve labeling and accessibility on all inpatient floors.
3. Confirm mixing valve temperature monitoring records for the water management program.
4. Confirm annual backflow test records and closure of failed-test work orders.
5. Review the utility failure log for trends and open corrective actions.
