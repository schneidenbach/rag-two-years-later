# SOP FAC-HVAC-021: Airborne Infection Isolation Room Pressure Monitoring

## Purpose of the isolation room pressure monitoring SOP

This procedure describes how Facilities Operations HVAC staff monitor, verify, and respond to alarms for airborne infection isolation (AII) rooms and protective environment (PE) rooms at Lakeshore Health. AII rooms must be maintained at negative pressure relative to the corridor to contain airborne pathogens; PE rooms are maintained at positive pressure to protect immunocompromised patients.

## Scope of isolation room pressure monitoring

FAC-HVAC-021 applies to every designated AII and PE room on North Campus, South Campus, and Riverside Campus, including the Emergency Department isolation rooms, inpatient isolation rooms, and the bronchoscopy suite. The list of designated rooms, with design pressure differential and air change rate, is maintained in the CMMS asset register.

## Isolation room pressure monitor equipment and alarm setpoints

Each designated isolation room has a room pressure monitor at the entrance with a local display, audible and visual alarm, and a connection to the building automation system (BAS). The alarm setpoint for AII rooms is a pressure differential less negative than -0.01 inches water column for more than 60 seconds. For PE rooms, the alarm setpoint is a differential less positive than +0.01 inches water column for more than 60 seconds. Alarm delays prevent nuisance alarms when the door is opened for entry.

## Daily verification of isolation room pressure when occupied

When an AII room is in use for an isolation patient, nursing staff perform a daily visual check of the monitor display and record it on the unit log. Facilities HVAC technicians review BAS trend data for all occupied isolation rooms each weekday morning and investigate any room that alarmed in the prior 24 hours. A tissue or smoke test at the door undercut is used to confirm direction of airflow when the monitor reading is in question.

## Monthly and annual isolation room pressure testing

Monthly, HVAC technicians verify monitor calibration against a reference manometer for each isolation room and record the result. Annually, the test and balance contractor verifies air change rates, exhaust flow, and pressure differential for each designated room. Results outside design tolerances generate a work order.

## Responding to an isolation room pressure alarm

A pressure alarm in an occupied AII room is a P1 Emergency under FAC-POL-002 and is dispatched directly to the HVAC on-call technician at any hour. The technician will:

1. Confirm the door is closed and the anteroom door (if present) is closed.
2. Check the monitor reading and the BAS for exhaust fan status and supply/exhaust damper positions.
3. Restore the exhaust fan or correct the damper fault, or reset the monitor if a sensor fault is confirmed.
4. If pressure cannot be restored within 30 minutes, notify the House Supervisor and Infection Prevention so the patient can be relocated to another AII room.

An alarm in an unoccupied isolation room is a P2 Urgent work order.

## Documentation of isolation room pressure monitoring

All alarms, verification checks, calibration results, and corrective actions are documented in the CMMS against the room asset. Infection Prevention receives a monthly report of isolation room alarms and out-of-tolerance findings for the Environment of Care Committee.
