# Memo: Cost of Inpatient Rooms Out of Service

**To:** Chief Operating Officer, Chief Nursing Officer, Director of Facilities Operations, Patient Flow / Bed Management
**From:** Renata Vos, Director of Financial Planning & Analysis
**Date:** March 16, 2026
**Subject:** Standard cost assumption for inpatient rooms taken out of service

## Purpose of the Finance room out of service cost memo

Operating leaders periodically ask Finance to estimate the financial impact of inpatient rooms that are blocked or taken out of service for maintenance, repair, or environmental reasons. This memo establishes a standard planning assumption for the cost of an inpatient room out of service at Lakeshore Health, to be used in capital requests, maintenance prioritization, and operational reviews. It replaces ad hoc estimates used in prior years.

## Standard cost per day of an inpatient room out of service

Based on FY25 actual volumes, payer mix, and average length of stay, each inpatient room out of service costs Lakeshore Health about **$2,150 per day** in lost contribution margin. For intensive care unit rooms, the cost is approximately **$4,800 per day** in lost contribution margin, reflecting higher acuity reimbursement and the limited availability of alternative ICU capacity. These figures represent contribution margin (net revenue less variable cost), not gross charges, and assume Lakeshore is operating at or near its typical occupancy, so that a blocked room displaces a patient rather than sitting idle.

## Basis and method for the room out of service cost estimate

The per-day figures were developed by dividing FY25 inpatient contribution margin by occupied patient days, separately for ICU and non-ICU units, then adjusting for the share of displaced patients who are diverted, transferred, or held in the Emergency Department. Holding patients in the ED also creates indirect costs (ED boarding, ambulance diversion hours, and reduced elective surgery throughput) that are not included in these figures, so the estimates should be treated as conservative.

## When rooms are taken out of service for maintenance conditions

Under the Patient Flow room status procedure, a patient room is taken out of service when a condition prevents safe occupancy. Common maintenance conditions that require taking a room out of service include:

- an inoperable toilet or flush valve in the patient bathroom;
- an inoperable medical gas outlet (oxygen, medical air, or vacuum) at the headwall, where a portable alternative is not acceptable for the patient population;
- an active water leak or sewage backup;
- loss of required pressure relationship in an airborne infection isolation room.

The room returns to service when Facilities Operations closes the work order and Environmental Services completes cleaning.

## Using the room out of service cost in maintenance prioritization

Facilities Operations and Supply Chain should use these figures when evaluating repair delays, parts stocking decisions, and vendor lead times. As an illustration of the method, a single ICU room held out of service for 14 calendar days while awaiting a repair part would cost approximately $67,200 in lost contribution margin (14 x $4,800). A non-ICU inpatient room out of service for the same period would cost approximately $30,100 (14 x $2,150). Decisions to reduce parts inventory or extend repair lead times should weigh these costs against the expected savings.

## Review cycle for the room out of service cost assumption

Finance will update the room out of service cost assumption annually following the close of each fiscal year, or sooner if payer mix or occupancy changes materially. Questions should be directed to the FP&A team.
