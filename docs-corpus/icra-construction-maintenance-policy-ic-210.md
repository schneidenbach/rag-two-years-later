# Policy IC-210: Infection Control Risk Assessment (ICRA) for Construction, Renovation, and Maintenance

## Purpose of the Lakeshore ICRA policy IC-210

This policy defines when an Infection Control Risk Assessment (ICRA) and ICRA permit are required before construction, renovation, or maintenance work in Lakeshore Health facilities, and the precautions that must be in place. The intent is to protect patients, particularly immunocompromised patients, from airborne and waterborne organisms such as Aspergillus and Legionella that can be released by dust, debris, or disturbed water systems.

## Scope of the ICRA policy for plumbing and maintenance work

IC-210 applies to all Facilities Operations staff, contractors, and vendors performing work in Lakeshore buildings. Routine plumbing work that does not disturb walls, ceilings, or floors (for example, replacing a faucet cartridge, rebuilding an exposed flush valve, or replacing a headwall outlet component without opening the wall) does not require an ICRA permit. **Plumbing work in patient care areas that requires wall or ceiling penetration requires an ICRA permit** before work begins. This includes opening a chase wall to access a concealed flush valve or supply, removing ceiling tiles above an occupied patient care area, and cutting into a headwall.

## ICRA risk classes for maintenance work in patient care areas

The ICRA class is determined from the type of work and the patient risk group of the area, using the Lakeshore ICRA matrix maintained by Infection Prevention.

- **Class I:** Inspection and non-invasive work, including removal of a single ceiling tile for visual inspection, limited to one tile per area.
- **Class II:** Small-scale, short-duration work that creates minimal dust, such as accessing a chase through an existing access panel.
- **Class III:** Work that generates moderate to high dust or requires demolition of a wall or ceiling section, requiring a sealed barrier, HEPA-filtered negative air, and covered transport of debris.
- **Class IV:** Major demolition or construction, requiring an anteroom, full barrier system, and Infection Prevention inspection before work begins.

Work requiring wall or ceiling penetration in intensive care units, operating rooms, and oncology units is designated **Class III or Class IV** because those areas house the highest-risk patient groups.

## ICRA permit request and turnaround process

The supervisor responsible for the work submits an ICRA permit request in the CMMS, attaching the work order, location, scope, expected duration, and proposed precautions. Infection Prevention reviews the request and issues the permit or requests changes. The **permit turnaround target is 1 business day** from complete submission. For P1 Emergency work, a verbal permit may be granted by the Infection Preventionist on call, with the written permit completed within one business day after the emergency.

## ICRA precautions and inspections during plumbing work

The technician posts the ICRA permit at the work area entrance. Required barriers, negative air machines, and walk-off mats are installed before penetration. Infection Prevention or a trained Facilities ICRA monitor inspects Class III and Class IV work areas at least daily. Work stops if barriers are breached. Following plumbing work that disturbs the potable water system in high-risk areas, the water management program may require flushing and temperature verification before the area returns to service.

## Closing an ICRA permit after maintenance work

The work area must be cleaned by Environmental Services and inspected before barriers are removed. The permit is closed in the CMMS by the supervisor, and the room is released to Patient Flow.

## ICRA permit volume and performance reporting

Infection Prevention reports permit volumes, turnaround times, and barrier compliance quarterly to the Environment of Care Committee. In FY26 to date, permit turnaround has generally met the 1 business day target; delays most often result from incomplete submissions.
