# SOP FAC-OPS-014: After-Hours Facilities Work Order Dispatch

## Purpose of the after-hours dispatch SOP FAC-OPS-014

This standard operating procedure defines how Facilities Operations work orders received after hours are routed, triaged, and assigned at all Lakeshore Health campuses (North Campus, South Campus, and Riverside Campus). It applies to work orders entered through the Facilities service desk phone line, the online request portal, and nurse station requests. Priority definitions referenced in this SOP are those in the Work Order Priority and Response Policy, FAC-POL-002.

## Definition of after hours for facilities dispatch

For purposes of FAC-OPS-014, after hours means 7:00 pm to 7:00 am every day, plus all hours on weekends and Lakeshore-observed holidays outside of staffed day shifts. The Facilities day shift begins at 7:00 am. During after hours, each campus is covered by an in-house General Maintenance (GM) technician on the night shift and by trade on-call technicians (plumbing, electrical, HVAC) who are available by page and respond from off-site unless already on site.

## After-hours routing of P1 Emergency work orders (Rev 4)

P1 Emergency work orders received after hours are paged **directly to the trade on-call** technician for the applicable trade (for example, the on-call plumber for a plumbing emergency). The service desk also notifies the House Supervisor and, for utility failures, the Plant Operations engineer on duty. The P1 response and completion targets in FAC-POL-002 apply (respond within 30 minutes; complete within 24 hours).

## After-hours routing of non-emergency P2 to P4 work orders (Rev 4)

Effective 2026-04-01, non-emergency after-hours work orders (priorities P2 Urgent, P3 Routine, and P4 Scheduled) are routed to the **General Maintenance on-call technician first**, rather than paging the trade on-call. The purpose of this change is to reduce trade overtime callouts for issues that can be resolved by GM staff. The General Maintenance technician will:

1. Respond to the location and triage the issue.
2. Resolve the issue if it is within GM scope (for example, resetting equipment, minor adjustments, making the area safe, placing signage or containment, or shutting off a local water supply).
3. If the issue is beyond GM scope, document the findings in the CMMS, make the area safe, notify the charge nurse, and leave the work order assigned to the GM queue.
4. At the start of the next day shift (7:00 am), the Facilities day supervisor reassigns tickets marked beyond GM scope to the appropriate trade.

The GM technician may escalate a ticket to P1 and page the trade on-call only if conditions change and the issue meets the P1 definition in FAC-POL-002.

## General Maintenance scope limits for after-hours plumbing work

General Maintenance technicians are **not licensed** for the following work, and these issues must be reassigned to a licensed plumber or certified installer:

- medical gas system repair, including zone valves and headwall outlets (oxygen, medical air, vacuum);
- backflow preventer repair and testing, including RPZ devices;
- flushometer and sensor flush valve repair, including solenoid and diaphragm replacement;
- thermostatic mixing valve adjustment or rebuild.

GM technicians may make these areas safe (for example, closing an isolation valve on a leaking fixture) but may not disassemble or repair the device.

## Rationale for Revision 4 of the after-hours dispatch SOP

Revision 4 was adopted to support the FY26 Facilities overtime budget reduction target of 18%. Trade on-call callouts for non-emergency work were identified as the largest controllable component of Facilities overtime in FY25. Routing non-emergency after-hours calls to the in-house GM technician, who is already on shift, is intended to avoid callout minimums and reduce trade overtime hours. The effect of this revision on response and completion times is to be reviewed at the Facilities Operations monthly review.

## Documentation requirements for after-hours facilities calls

All after-hours calls must be entered in the CMMS before dispatch. The responding technician records arrival time, findings, actions taken, and whether the issue is within scope. Tickets reassigned from GM to a trade must show the reassignment as a separate action with the receiving trade and time. Utility failures affecting patient care must also be documented in the utility failure log per the Environment of Care utility management plan.
