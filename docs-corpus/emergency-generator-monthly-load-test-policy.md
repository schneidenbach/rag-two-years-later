# Policy FAC-POL-015: Emergency Generator Monthly Load Testing

## Purpose of the emergency generator load test policy

This policy establishes requirements for monthly load testing of emergency generators and automatic transfer switches (ATS) that supply the essential electrical system at Lakeshore Health. Regular testing under load confirms that emergency power will be available to life safety, critical, and equipment branches during a utility outage.

## Scope of emergency generator testing at Lakeshore campuses

FAC-POL-015 applies to all emergency generators and transfer switches serving inpatient and ambulatory buildings: the North Campus Central Plant generators serving Tower A, Tower B, and the Heart & Vascular Pavilion; the South Hospital generators serving South Hospital and the Women's & Children's Pavilion; and the Riverside Hospital generators serving Riverside Hospital and the Riverside Outpatient Center. Generators serving medical office buildings that do not support patient care are tested under the manufacturer's schedule.

## Monthly emergency generator load test requirements

Each emergency generator is tested at least 12 times per year, with intervals of not less than 20 days and not more than 40 days between tests. Each monthly test runs for at least 30 continuous minutes under a dynamic load that is at least 30% of the generator nameplate rating, or under a load that achieves the manufacturer's recommended exhaust gas temperature. If the building load is insufficient, a supplemental load bank is used. Monthly testing is scheduled as P4 Scheduled work in the CMMS.

## Automatic transfer switch testing during the monthly generator test

During each monthly test, every automatic transfer switch served by the generator is operated to transfer the load from normal power to emergency power. The technician records the time for the generator to start, reach rated voltage and frequency, and accept the load. Transfer to the life safety and critical branches must occur within 10 seconds of normal power loss.

## Coordination and notification for generator load tests

Plant Operations publishes the monthly test schedule two weeks in advance. The House Supervisor, Clinical Engineering, Information Services, and Surgical Services are notified at least 24 hours before each test. Tests are not performed during active surgical cases in rooms served by an ATS being transferred, unless Surgical Services agrees. A test is postponed if severe weather is forecast, and rescheduled within the 40-day interval.

## Documentation of generator load test results

For each test, the technician records the date, run time, load in kilowatts and percent of nameplate, exhaust temperature, oil pressure, coolant temperature, fuel level, ATS transfer times, and any abnormal conditions. Records are kept in the CMMS and are available for survey. Any failure to start, failure to transfer within 10 seconds, or failure to carry load is documented as a utility failure and reported to the Environment of Care Committee.

## Response to a failed emergency generator load test

A failed load test generates a P1 Emergency work order. Plant Operations arranges a portable generator if the failed unit cannot be returned to service within four hours, and notifies the Director of Facilities Operations and the Emergency Management Coordinator. Interim life safety measures are assessed for the affected buildings.

## Annual and triennial generator testing

In addition to monthly testing, each generator receives an annual load bank test and a triennial four-hour continuous run test, scheduled by Plant Operations with the generator service contractor.
